1. Core Principles

* **Divergent Before Convergent Thinking:** Separate the generation of ideas from their evaluation. Mix both simultaneously to prevent premature criticism.
* **Defer Judgment:** Encourage wild or unconventional ideas early. Radical concepts often refine into breakthrough solutions.
* **Aim for Quantity:** Generating a high volume of ideas increases the probability of discovering novel, high-impact concepts.
* **Build on Others:** Use "Yes, and..." thinking to expand, recombining, and upgrading existing concepts rather than shooting them down with "No, but..."
* **Visualizing Thoughts:** Use diagrams, sketches, and sticky notes to lower cognitive load and improve clarity across team members.

## 2. Key Ideation Techniques

### SCAMPER Framework

A systematic checklist used to prompt alternative perspectives on an existing product, service, or problem:

* **Substitute:** What materials, steps, people, or components can be swapped?
* **Combine:** Can two distinct features, services, or processes be merged?
* **Adapt:** What existing solutions from other domains or industries apply here?
* **Modify / Magnify:** What happens if an attribute is scaled up to an extreme?
* **Put to Another Use:** How can this product or service solve a completely different problem?
* **Eliminate:** What elements can be stripped away to simplify the core offering?
* **Reverse / Rearrange:** What happens if the current workflow or order is flipped?

### Reverse Brainstorming

Instead of asking "How do we solve this problem?", ask **"How could we deliberately cause or worsen this problem?"**

1. List all the ways to fail or create friction for the user.
2. Analyze the negative list to identify underlying risks or current vulnerabilities.
3. Flip each negative factor into a practical, preventive solution.

### Brainwriting (6-3-5 Method)

A quiet, written alternative to verbal sessions that prevents louder participants from dominating:

* **6 Participants:** Each receives a worksheet.
* **3 Ideas:** Each participant writes down 3 ideas within 5 minutes.
* **5 Passes:** Sheets are passed to the person on the right. Each participant builds upon or adds to the ideas on the paper for 5 consecutive rounds.

### Six Thinking Hats (Edward de Bono)

Rotate the team through structured perspective "hats" to view an idea from all angles:

* **White Hat (Data):** Focus on facts, existing metrics, and missing information.
* **Red Hat (Emotion):** Express gut feelings, intuition, and raw reactions without needing justification.
* **Black Hat (Caution):** Evaluate risks, flaws, security issues, and worst-case scenarios.
* **Yellow Hat (Optimism):** Identify benefits, potential value, and best-case scenarios.
* **Green Hat (Creativity):** Generate wild alternatives, fresh concepts, and unconventional paths.
* **Blue Hat (Process):** Manage the session structure, timekeeping, and action items.

### Crazy Eights

A rapid sketching sprint commonly used in Design Sprints:

* Give each participant an A4 paper folded into 8 sections.
* Set a timer for **8 minutes**.
* Every participant must sketch **8 distinct ideas** (1 minute per panel).
* Focus on visual concepts rather than artistic quality.

## 3. Ideation Session Workflow

```
[Phase 1: Problem Definition] ➔ [Phase 2: Warm-up & Setup] ➔ [Phase 3: Divergent Generation] ➔ [Phase 4: Convergent Filtering] ➔ [Phase 5: Next Steps]

```

1. **Problem Definition (10 min):** Frame the challenge using "How Might We" (HMW) statements.
2. **Warm-up (5 min):** Run a quick low-stakes exercise (e.g., "Alternative uses for a paperclip") to loosen creative thinking.
3. **Divergent Generation (25 min):** Execute 1–2 structured techniques (e.g., Silent Brainwriting + SCAMPER).
4. **Group Clustering (15 min):** Group similar sticky notes or sketches into logical themes.
5. **Convergent Filtering (15 min):** Apply selection matrices or dot-voting to isolate high-potential ideas.

## 4. Evaluation & Prioritization Matrix

### Impact vs. Effort Matrix

|  | Low Effort | High Effort |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **High Impact** | **Quick Wins** (Execute immediately) | **Strategic Bets** (Plan and resource carefully) |
| **Low Impact** | **Fill-ins** (Delegate or automate) | **Thankless Tasks** (Discard/Deprioritize) |

### Dot Voting

* Give each team member **3 to 5 sticky dots** (or digital markers).
* Allow participants to vote on their top choices silently to prevent bandwagon effects.
* Set explicit voting criteria beforehand (e.g., "Vote for the most feasible idea" or "Vote for the most innovative idea").

## 5. Facilitation Best Practices

* **Establish Psychological Safety:** Clarify upfront that all ideas are valid during generation phases.
* **Enforce Timeboxes:** Keep timers visible to maintain energy and prevent over-analyzing single ideas.
* **Maintain Asynchronous Options:** Use digital whiteboards (Miro, FigJam, Mural) so remote participants contribute on equal footing.
* **Record Context:** Document not just the final winning ideas, but also the underlying *why* behind rejected or deferred concepts.
